ETERNAL SECURITY

Can I Lose My Salvation? What the Bible Says About Eternal Security

By In Him Daily  ·  inhimdaily.org

This is one of the most searched theological questions on the internet — and one of the most personally urgent. It is not asked by people who are casually curious about systematic theology. It is asked by people who sinned badly after becoming a Christian and are lying awake at night wondering whether what they received has been taken back.

This article takes that question seriously. It does not flatten the biblical tension in either direction — pretending the warning texts do not exist, or pretending the security texts do not exist. Both are in the Bible. Both must be heard. And the resolution, when it comes, does not rest on the believer's performance. It rests on Christ's faithfulness.

The Question Behind the Question

When someone searches "can I lose my salvation," they are rarely asking a theological question in the abstract. They are asking one of these specific personal questions:

"I committed a serious sin after becoming a Christian. Does that mean I was never truly saved, or that I have now lost what I had?"

"I have been living far from God for years. Has my persistent disobedience cancelled my salvation?"

"I have serious doubts. I am not sure I believe what I used to believe. Does doubt mean I have fallen away?"

The fear underneath all of these is the same: that my standing before God depends on the consistency and quality of my own performance — and that I have not been consistent or good enough to maintain it.

This fear is the precise fear that the New Testament's security texts are designed to address.

The Security Texts — What They Actually Say

The strongest statements of eternal security in the New Testament are not vague reassurances. They are precise, carefully worded declarations that deliberately exclude every possible threat to the believer's standing.

John 10:28-29 — "I give them eternal life, and they shall never perish; no one will snatch them out of my hand. My Father, who has given them to me, is greater than all; no one can snatch them out of my Father's hand."

Four things to note in these two verses. First: the life given is eternal — by definition, life that ends is not eternal. Second: they shall never perish — the Greek uses a double negative (ou me) which is the strongest possible negation in the language. They shall absolutely never perish. Third: no one can snatch them from the Son's hand. Fourth: no one can snatch them from the Father's hand. The believer is held in two hands simultaneously — the Son's and the Father's. Both omnipotent. Both absolutely reliable. The question is not whether the hands are strong enough. They are. The question is whether anything can remove the believer from them.

Romans 8:38-39 — "I am convinced that neither death nor life, neither angels nor demons, neither the present nor the future, nor any powers, neither height nor depth, nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord."

Paul exhausts the categories of possible threat. Death cannot. Life cannot. Angels, demons, powers cannot. Past, present, future cannot. Height, depth cannot. Anything else in all creation — he adds this as a catch-all to ensure nothing is left out. The love of God in Christ Jesus cannot be separated from those who are in him.

Ephesians 1:13-14 — "Having believed, you were marked in him with a seal, the promised Holy Spirit, who is a deposit guaranteeing our inheritance until the redemption of those who are God's possession."

The Holy Spirit is the seal of the believer's eternal inheritance — a deposit guaranteeing the final redemption. A seal in the ancient world was the mark of ownership and protection. The Spirit's presence in the believer is God's own guarantee that the inheritance will be fully delivered.

Hebrews 7:25 — "He is able to save completely those who come to God through him, because he always lives to intercede for them." Completely. Always. The salvation offered is not partial or provisional. It is complete — and the mechanism that sustains it is Christ's permanent, unceasing intercession, not the believer's consistent performance.

The Warning Texts — What They Actually Say

Intellectual honesty requires that we also hear the warning texts — the passages that call believers to perseverance and warn of the consequences of falling away. They are real. They are serious. And they cannot be read away by simply declaring that every warning is hypothetical.

Hebrews 6:4-6 — "It is impossible for those who have once been enlightened, who have tasted the heavenly gift, who have shared in the Holy Spirit, who have tasted the goodness of the word of God and the powers of the coming age and who have fallen away, to be brought back to repentance."

Hebrews 10:26-27 — "If we deliberately keep on sinning after we have received the knowledge of the truth, no sacrifice for sins is left, but only a fearful expectation of judgment and of raging fire that will consume the enemies of God."

2 Peter 2:20-21 — "If they have escaped the corruption of the world by knowing our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ and are again entangled in it and are overcome, they are worse off at the end than they were at the beginning."

These texts are sobering. They must not be domesticated. But neither must they be read as if they are the only voice in the New Testament on this subject.

Holding the Tension — The New Covenant Resolution

The tension between the security texts and the warning texts has produced two major theological traditions. Calvinist/Reformed theology holds that the elect cannot finally fall away — the warning texts are addressed to those who were never truly saved or serve as means by which God preserves the elect. Arminian theology holds that genuine believers can apostatise — the warning texts describe real possibilities for real believers, and genuine faith can be genuinely abandoned.

Both traditions include serious, Scripture-honouring theologians. This article does not attempt to settle the debate between them. What it does is offer the resolution that the New Covenant framework provides — a resolution that takes both the security texts and the warning texts with full seriousness.

The New Covenant security does not rest on the believer's performance. It rests on Christ's faithfulness.

Hebrews 10:23 — "Let us hold unswervingly to the hope we profess, for he who promised is faithful." The ground of holding on is not our grip strength. It is the faithfulness of the one who promised. We hold on because he holds us. Jude 24 — "To him who is able to keep you from stumbling and to present you before his glorious presence without fault and with great joy." He keeps. He presents. The active verbs belong to him.

The warning texts, in this framework, function as the means by which genuine believers are preserved — not as threats that genuine belief might be insufficient to sustain. The person who reads Hebrews 6 and is sobered by it is not the person who has fallen away. The person who has fallen away does not fear the warning. The sober reading of the warning is itself evidence of ongoing genuine faith.

The Specific Fear — Answered Directly

To the person who sinned badly after becoming a Christian and is wondering whether they are still saved:

1 John 2:1 — "My dear children, I write this to you so that you will not sin. But if anybody does sin, we have an advocate with the Father — Jesus Christ, the Righteous One."

John wrote this to believers. He anticipated that believers would sin — "if anybody does sin." And the provision for that sin is not the loss of salvation. It is an Advocate — Jesus Christ, the Righteous One, who intercedes before the Father on behalf of the sinning believer.

The question "did I lose my salvation when I sinned?" assumes that salvation is maintained by not sinning. But if that were true, no believer would be saved for long. Salvation is maintained by the intercession of the one who is always righteous, always before the Father, always pleading the sufficiency of his own sacrifice for every sin the believer commits.

Romans 8:1 — "Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus." No condemnation — not: no condemnation for those who sin less frequently. No condemnation — for those who are in Christ Jesus.

The Warning That Matters

The warning texts in Hebrews and 2 Peter are addressed to people who have heard the gospel, experienced its initial effects, and then turned their backs on it entirely — not to people who are sinning and afraid and crying out to God.

The person who is afraid they have lost their salvation — who is reading this article because they sinned badly and want to know if they are still held — is showing the marks of genuine faith. Fear of falling away, genuine repentance, ongoing concern for standing before God — these are not the marks of someone who has finally and fully apostatised. They are the marks of someone who is being preserved.

Philippians 1:6 — "I am confident of this, that he who began a good work in you will carry it on to completion until the day of Christ Jesus." The one who began it carries it to completion. The believer who is stumbling, struggling, failing, and returning is not outside the reach of the one who carries that work to its completion. They are inside it.
