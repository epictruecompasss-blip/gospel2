THE HOLY SPIRIT

Who Is the Holy Spirit? What the Bible Says

By In Him Daily  ·  inhimdaily.org

The Holy Spirit may be the most misunderstood person in Christianity. He has been reduced to a feeling — a warm sensation in a worship service. He has been marketed as a power to be activated through the right formula or the right giving. He has been turned into a theological abstraction — the third member of the Trinity discussed in doctrine but rarely encountered in life.

The Bible's picture of the Holy Spirit is completely different from all of these. He is a person — not a force, not a feeling, not a spiritual commodity. He is the third person of the Trinity, equal in divinity to the Father and the Son, and his arrival at Pentecost was not a bonus feature of the gospel. It was the point. It was what Jesus said was better than his own physical presence.

This article traces what the Bible actually says about who the Holy Spirit is and what he does.

The Holy Spirit Is a Person — Not a Force

The most foundational truth about the Holy Spirit is one that is constantly obscured: he is a person. Not an it. Not a force or an energy or a spiritual atmosphere. A person — with mind, will, emotions, and the capacity for relationship.

The New Testament establishes the Holy Spirit's personhood through the things it says he does. Persons do these things. Forces do not.

He grieves. Ephesians 4:30 — "Do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God, with whom you were sealed for the day of redemption." You cannot grieve a force. Grief requires the capacity for relationship and the experience of relational pain. The Holy Spirit can be grieved because he is in a genuine relationship with the believer — a relationship that our sin damages.

He speaks. Acts 13:2 — "While they were worshipping the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit said, 'Set apart for me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them.'" The Holy Spirit gave specific, directive communication to a specific group of people. This is not the diffuse influence of an atmosphere. This is the intentional speech of a person.

He intercedes. Romans 8:26-27 — "The Spirit himself intercedes for us through wordless groans... the Spirit intercedes for God's people in accordance with the will of God." The Holy Spirit prays. He takes our inarticulate longings, our groanings that cannot be formed into words, and intercedes for us with the Father. Only a person can intercede.

He decides. 1 Corinthians 12:11 — "All these are the work of one and the same Spirit, and he distributes them to each one, just as he determines." The Holy Spirit makes decisions about how to distribute spiritual gifts. Not randomly, not mechanically, but according to his own determination.

He can be lied to. Acts 5:3 — Peter said to Ananias, "How is it that Satan has so filled your heart that you have lied to the Holy Spirit?" You cannot lie to a force. You can only lie to a person.

The Holy Spirit in the Old Testament

The Holy Spirit is not a New Testament invention. He was present and active throughout the Old Testament — but his mode of activity was different from what would come at Pentecost.

In Genesis 1:2, "the Spirit of God was hovering over the waters" at creation. The Spirit was present at the beginning of all things.

Throughout the Old Testament the Spirit comes upon specific people for specific tasks. He comes upon the craftsmen who built the tabernacle, giving them skill and creativity (Exodus 31:3). He comes upon the judges — Othniel, Gideon, Samson — empowering them for military deliverance. He comes upon the prophets, enabling them to speak the word of God.

But the Old Testament Spirit-empowerment was consistently temporary and task-specific. The Spirit came upon Samson for a task and, when Samson's consecration was broken, departed — and Samson "did not know that the Lord had left him" (Judges 16:20). The Spirit's departure was possible under the Old Covenant.

And the prophets looked forward to something different. Joel 2:28 — "I will pour out my Spirit on all people. Your sons and daughters will prophesy, your old men will dream dreams, your young men will see visions." Not some people. Not for specific tasks. All people. This was the promise that Pentecost fulfilled.

What Changed at Pentecost

John 16:7 contains one of the most surprising statements Jesus ever made: "It is for your good that I am going away. Unless I go away, the Advocate will not come to you; but if I go, I will send him to you."

Jesus told his disciples that his departure was better for them than his continued physical presence. This is a staggering claim. How could the absence of Jesus be better than his presence?

The answer is the Holy Spirit. While Jesus was physically present, he was limited by geography — he could only be in one place at one time. When the Holy Spirit was poured out at Pentecost, the presence of God became universally accessible — not limited to Palestine, not limited to one generation, not limited to those who could physically travel to where Jesus was.

Acts 2:1-4 records the arrival: "When the day of Pentecost came, they were all together in one place. Suddenly a sound like the blowing of a violent wind came from heaven and filled the whole house where they were sitting... All of them were filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other tongues as the Spirit enabled them."

Peter's sermon that followed explains what had happened: "This is what was spoken by the prophet Joel" (Acts 2:16). The promise of Joel 2 had been fulfilled. The permanent, universal indwelling of the Spirit had arrived. And it was available to everyone who responded to the gospel: "Repent and be baptised, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins. And you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. The promise is for you and your children and for all who are far off" (Acts 2:38-39).

What the Holy Spirit Does

The New Testament describes the Holy Spirit's work across five dimensions — each one crucial to understanding what it means to live the Christian life.

Conviction. John 16:8 — "When he comes, he will prove the world to be in the wrong about sin and righteousness and judgment." The Holy Spirit is the one who produces genuine conviction of sin — not guilt that paralyses but conviction that turns. No amount of preaching produces genuine conviction of sin without the Spirit's work. He opens the heart to see what the mind alone cannot perceive.

New Birth. John 3:5-6 — "No one can enter the kingdom of God unless they are born of water and the Spirit. Flesh gives birth to flesh, but the Spirit gives birth to spirit." The new birth — the moment of genuine Christian conversion — is the work of the Holy Spirit. He is not the reward for conversion. He is the agent of it.

Indwelling. 1 Corinthians 6:19 — "Do you not know that your bodies are temples of the Holy Spirit, who is in you, whom you have received from God?" Every believer is indwelt by the Holy Spirit. Not just the spiritually mature. Not just those who have had certain experiences. Every person who has genuinely received Christ has the Holy Spirit dwelling within them. This is the permanent mode of the new covenant — not the temporary Spirit-empowerment of the Old Testament judges but the continuous, unbroken indwelling of God himself within the believer.

Gifting. 1 Corinthians 12:4-7 — "There are different kinds of gifts, but the same Spirit distributes them... to each one the manifestation of the Spirit is given for the common good." The Holy Spirit gives each believer gifts for the building up of the whole community. These gifts are not performances or spiritual achievements. They are manifestations of the Spirit's presence distributed according to his own determination for the benefit of the whole body.

Sealing. Ephesians 1:13-14 — "Having believed, you were marked in him with a seal, the promised Holy Spirit, who is a deposit guaranteeing our inheritance until the redemption of those who are God's possession." The Holy Spirit is the seal of the believer's eternal inheritance — the guarantee that what God has begun he will complete. The presence of the Holy Spirit in the believer is not an optional extra. He is the down payment of eternal life.

What the Holy Spirit Is Not

In a generation where the Holy Spirit has been extensively marketed, it is necessary to say what the Bible does not say.

The Holy Spirit is not a commodity to be purchased or activated through giving. Simon the Sorcerer tried to buy the ability to impart the Spirit through money and was told in the sharpest terms: "May your money perish with you, because you thought you could buy the gift of God with money!" (Acts 8:20).

The Holy Spirit is not the reward for sufficient spiritual performance. He is a gift — freely given to those who receive Christ. Acts 2:38 calls him "the gift of the Holy Spirit." Gifts are not earned. They are received.

The Holy Spirit is not primarily a feeling. His presence is not confirmed by emotional experience — though he does produce genuine emotion. His presence is confirmed by the fruit he produces: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control (Galatians 5:22-23). These are the marks of the Spirit's presence — not euphoria, not tongues, not physical manifestation, but the steady, persistent transformation of character.

The Spirit and the Son

The Holy Spirit never draws attention to himself. His consistent work is to glorify Jesus. John 16:14 — "He will glorify me because it is from me that he will receive what he will make known to you." Any Spirit movement that directs attention away from Jesus and toward itself, toward the manifestations, toward the leaders who claim to channel the Spirit's power — has departed from what the Bible describes.

The Holy Spirit is the one who opens our eyes to see Christ clearly, to understand the Scriptures as testimonies to Christ (Luke 24:45), and to live the life that Christ's resurrection makes possible. He is the Spirit of Christ (Romans 8:9) — and his presence in the believer is the presence of Christ in the believer, mediated through the third person of the Trinity.

The promise of Acts 2:38 is still open: repent, be baptised, receive forgiveness, receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. The gift is not earned. It is not purchased. It is not unlocked through spiritual technique. It is received — by everyone who turns to the Son and is immersed into his name.
