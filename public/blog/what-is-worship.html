What Is Worship? More Than a Song

By Sir Henry M M  ·  In Him Daily

What Is Worship? More Than a Song

"I feel nothing during worship services. What am I doing wrong?"

This is one of the most quietly common experiences in the church. People stand in a room where others appear to be having a profound spiritual experience. The music is playing. The words are on the screen. And inside — nothing. Or something that feels manufactured — the effort to feel something that is not naturally present. The experience of performing worship rather than doing it.

If this is your experience, you are not alone. And the reason it is so common is that the word "worship" has been narrowed, in most modern church contexts, to mean a specific musical activity that happens for twenty to thirty minutes before the sermon. That narrowing has produced enormous confusion — because if worship is primarily a musical feeling, then the person who does not feel the feeling during the music is left wondering what is wrong with them.

Nothing is wrong with them. The definition is wrong.

What the Word Actually Means

The most common Greek word translated "worship" in the New Testament is proskuneo — to bow toward, to prostrate oneself before, to turn the whole body in the direction of the one being honoured. It appears 59 times in the New Testament. Not once does it refer to singing.

Proskuneo is the word used when the Magi "bowed down and worshipped" the infant Jesus (Matthew 2:11). It is the word used when Thomas saw the risen Christ and said "My Lord and my God!" (John 20:28). It is the word used in Revelation 4-5 when the elders and the living creatures bow before the throne. In every case, the word describes a posture — the whole person oriented toward the one who is worthy of the orientation.

The second important Greek word is latreia — service, the dedicated activity of the person whose life is devoted to a particular end. Romans 12:1 uses this word: "Therefore, I urge you, brothers and sisters, in view of God's mercy, to offer your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God — this is your true and proper latreia." Your true and proper worship — the word Paul uses — is offering your body as a living sacrifice. Not a musical feeling. A life.

These two words together give the complete biblical picture: worship is the whole person (body, mind, will, emotion) oriented toward God — both in specific acts of directed honour (proskuneo) and in the ongoing offering of the whole life as a sacrifice of devotion (latreia).

John 4:23-24 — The Conversation That Defines Everything

The most important single passage on worship in the New Testament is a conversation by a well in Samaria.

John 4:19-24 — the Samaritan woman, after recognising that Jesus is a prophet, raises the most pressing worship question of her day: "Our ancestors worshipped on this mountain, but you Jews claim that the place where we must worship is in Jerusalem." The question is about location — which mountain produces the authentic worship? Gerizim or Zion?

Jesus' answer reframes the entire question: "Woman, believe me, a time is coming when you will worship the Father neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem... a time is coming and has now come when the true worshippers will worship the Father in the Spirit and in truth, for they are the kind of worshippers the Father seeks. God is Spirit, and his worshippers must worship in the Spirit and in truth."

Three things from this passage that permanently redefine worship.

First: the location question is answered. True worship is not tied to a place — not to Jerusalem, not to Gerizim, not to a particular building or style of meeting. The presence of God is no longer mediated through a geographic location. The Spirit, poured out at Pentecost, makes every location equally accessible to the Father's presence.

Second: the Father is seeking worshippers. Not waiting for adequately performed worship. Seeking worshippers. The initiative is the Father's. He is the one who draws people toward genuine worship — which means the problem of "feeling nothing" during music is not primarily your problem to solve. It is an invitation to understand what the Father is actually seeking.

Third: in the Spirit and in truth. These two qualifiers have been debated but their core meaning is clear. "In the Spirit" — worship that is animated by, enabled by, and expressed through the Holy Spirit who indwells the believer. Not the performance of religious ritual by unassisted human effort. "In truth" — worship that corresponds to who God actually is, not a God of our own imagining. Worship rooted in the revelation of God's character in Scripture and supremely in Christ.

Together these two qualifiers describe worship that is genuine — not manufactured feeling, not performance, not the right musical style. The Spirit enables. The truth grounds. And what emerges is the orientation of the whole person toward the God who is actually there.

Romans 12:1-2 — The Worship That Is a Life

Romans 12:1-2 is the fullest statement of what worship looks like as a life rather than an event:

"Therefore, I urge you, brothers and sisters, in view of God's mercy, to offer your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God — this is your true and proper worship. Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind. Then you will be able to test and approve what God's will is — his good, pleasing and perfect will."

Three elements of this verse that change everything.

"In view of God's mercy." The motivation for worship is not duty, not obligation, not the desire to earn favour. It is the received mercy of God — the grace that has already arrived, the forgiveness already given, the Spirit already indwelling. Worship flows from what has been received, not toward what is being earned.

"Offer your bodies as a living sacrifice." The Old Testament sacrificial system offered dead animals. The New Covenant offers a living sacrifice — the whole person, alive, in the ordinary activities of daily life. The Monday morning commute, the parenting of children, the use of money, the exercise of vocational gifts, the conversation with the difficult colleague — all of it offered to God as an act of worship. The narrowing of worship to Sunday music is not just incomplete. It is a shrinkage of what God has called his people to.

"Transformed by the renewing of your mind." Worship that is genuine produces transformation. The person who is truly oriented toward God — whose life is genuinely offered as a living sacrifice — is a person who is being changed. The transformation is the fruit of the worship, not the condition for it. You do not have to be transformed enough to worship. You are transformed by worshipping.

The Problem With Worship as Feeling

The reduction of worship to musical feeling has produced several specific problems that the biblical definition resolves.

The performance problem. When worship is a feeling, the worshipper is trying to generate it — through the right music, the right atmosphere, the right level of emotional preparation. This makes worship fundamentally about the worshipper's interior state rather than about the God who is being worshipped. It shifts the attention from the object of worship to the experience of the worshipper.

The inconsistency problem. Feelings are inconsistent. The person who has a profound experience in one worship service will not have the same experience in the next. If worship is the feeling, then the person is worshipping sometimes and not worshipping at other times — depending on factors as unpredictable as how well they slept the night before. This inconsistency produces the guilt of "I'm not worshipping well enough" that so many people carry.

The accessibility problem. Musical feelings are not equally accessible to all people. Personality, cultural background, neurological wiring, current emotional state — all of these affect how a person responds to a particular musical style. The introvert in a high-energy praise service is not worshipping less than the extrovert who is visibly moved. They may be worshipping more deeply — offering the whole person toward God in the midst of a context that feels alien — if we use the biblical definition.

The depth problem. If worship is a feeling produced by music, then the Christian life has a depth ceiling. The deepest spiritual experience available is the feeling. But if worship is the orientation of the whole person toward God — in prayer, in Scripture, in service, in suffering, in creativity, in vocation, in the Lord's Supper, in the gathered community, and yes, in song — then the depth is inexhaustible. The music is one expression of worship, not the whole of it.

What Genuine Worship Produces

Revelation 4-5 gives the New Testament's fullest picture of genuine worship — and it is not a worship service. It is a throne room. The four living creatures and the twenty-four elders are not producing a feeling. They are responding to a reality: the worthiness of the one on the throne.

Revelation 4:11 — "You are worthy, our Lord and God, to receive glory and honour and power, for you created all things, and by your will they were created and have their being." Revelation 5:12 — "Worthy is the Lamb, who was slain, to receive power and wealth and wisdom and strength and honour and glory and praise!" The worship of the throne room is not generated by musical atmosphere. It is the spontaneous, irresistible response of those who see who he is.

This is the direction the biblical definition of worship points. Not: how do I produce the feeling? But: how do I see more clearly who he is? Because when the vision of God becomes more vivid — through Scripture, through prayer, through the Lord's Supper, through genuine encounter — worship follows as naturally and inevitably as the elders casting their crowns.

C.S. Lewis put it memorably: "I think we delight to praise what we enjoy because the praise not merely expresses but completes the enjoyment; it is its appointed consummation." Worship completes the enjoyment of God. And the person who has genuinely tasted who God is will find that worship — comprehensive, whole-life, Spirit-enabled, truth-grounded worship — is not something they must force. It is something that overflows.
